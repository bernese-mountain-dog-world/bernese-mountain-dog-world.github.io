How Much Do Bernese Mountain Dogs Eat? Real Owner Feeding Amounts by Body Weight, Age and Activity vs the Kibble Bag Chart

Home›Guides›Bernese feeding portions

How much do Bernese Mountain Dogs eat? In one owner discussion, a 75-lb senior got 2½ cups a day, a 100-lb adolescent got 4 cups of dry food plus snacks, and a 105-lb dog got 3½ cups on a usual day. Those figures describe different dogs and foods. For your own Berner, read the daily range on the exact bag you're using and compare it with what goes into the bowl, including extras. What do Bernese owners feed by weight and age?

The owner reports below give you a feel for how different a day’s ration can look. The closest weight matches are worth reading together: a nearly two-year-old just over 90 lb got about 3½ cups, while a 90-lb dog in the same thread got 6 cups of a different food. One owner gave an age and the other didn’t. There’s no way to tell from those two posts how much of the difference came from the recipe, age, treats or the dog’s daily routine.

What does the kibble bag say at those weights?

The example bag places a 90-lb adult in a range of 3⅔–4½ cups a day. At 110 lb the same label works out to 4¾ cups. The 110-lb owner’s reported 4–6 cups straddles that benchmark by volume. Since the owner didn’t identify the food, there’s no reliable calorie comparison.

The calorie column multiplies the printed cup amounts by the printed energy per cup and rounds to the nearest whole calorie. The label lists approximately 106 g per cup. If your packet gives kcal per kilogram, divide that figure by 1,000 for kcal per gram and multiply by the weight you serve. This lets you repeat the same portion with a kitchen scale even if someone else normally pours the food.

Does more exercise mean a bigger dinner?

Look at the pattern of work and the dog’s condition over time. The MSD Veterinary Manual’s feeding guidance says energy needs can rise with the extent of work and calls for adjusting estimates based on observations including weight, condition and performance. The 105-lb example shows how one household varied an amount between usual and very active days. The owner also gave treats, so the extra half-cup tells only part of the story.

How do age and body condition change how you read the chart?

Use a large-breed growth formula and its own feeding instructions for a growing Berner. The veterinary guidance above ties the move off a growth diet to skeletal maturity. An adolescent in our table weighed 100 lb, which also appears on the adult bag. That overlap in weight alone tells you little about which food or chart applies. If you’re unsure which row to use or when to switch, bring the label and your puppy’s recent weights to your vet. The Berner adolescent guide covers other routines you may be changing around the same time.

For a senior, make a note of what has changed since the previous weigh-in. Compare how much you offer with how much the dog finishes. Include food from anyone else in the household. The veterinary manual describes both excess weight and declining condition in older dogs, so a smaller scoop is a poor default for every senior.

Follow the WSAVA body condition chart to check the ribs, waist from above and upward tuck behind the ribs from the side. Pair that check with repeat weigh-ins. Bring a record to the vet if you see an unexplained change in weight, condition or appetite.

Bernese feeding FAQ

Is 4 cups enough for a 100-lb Bernese Mountain Dog?

Four cups falls inside the 76–100 lb range on the example adult label above. Whether it works for your Berner depends on the exact food, what else the dog eats and how their weight and condition change. Check the chart on your own bag and count the full day’s food.

Do I use the same chart for my puppy and adult?

Use the growth formula’s directions for a growing large-breed dog. In the owner discussion, a 100-lb dog was only 11 months old. A weight you recognize from an adult chart does not establish that a puppy is ready for adult food. Bring up the switch at a growth check.

Does the bag mean cups per meal or per day?

The example label lists a daily amount. If the row you’re using says 4½ cups per day and you split it evenly into two meals, that is 2¼ cups at each meal. Check the units and time period on your packet before measuring out breakfast.

Can I compare two kibbles using the same scoop?

You can compare volume that way. To compare energy, multiply what that scoop holds in standard cups by each product’s kcal per cup. The adult example is 407 kcal per cup, so keeping the scoop unchanged only keeps the calorie amount unchanged if the new food has the same energy per cup.

Should I add a half-cup after a long walk?

The half-cup difference in the chart came from one 105-lb dog’s owner. Start by noting whether these longer outings have become routine, counting training treats and checking weight and condition over time. Use that record and your dog’s actual food when discussing a portion change with your vet.
