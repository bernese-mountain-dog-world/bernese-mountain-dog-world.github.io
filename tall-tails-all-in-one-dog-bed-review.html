Tall Tails All-in-One Dog Bed Review: Does the XL Fit a Bernese Mountain Dog?

Sizes, the three setups, washing rules and a fit checker, so you know before you order whether 44 inches is enough for your Berner.

Anna Lindqvist · Bernese Mountain Dog World

This Tall Tails All-in-One Dog Bed review answers one question first: does the XL actually fit a grown Bernese Mountain Dog? On paper the XL measures 44 by 34 inches, and an adult Berner is a big, long dog, so the answer depends on how your dog sleeps more than on the size label. Below you can check your own dog in a minute, then read how the three setups, the washing rules and the price work out for a giant, shedding breed.

Quick verdict: The XL is a good fit for a Berner who curls up or sleeps with their back pressed against a wall, and for any Berner who likes the removable cushion as a second bed in the car or on the couch. A big male who sleeps flat on his side with legs straight out may find 44 inches tight, especially once the bolster takes its share. Measure first: nose to the base of the tail, plus room to stretch. Our spec-based score is 3.6 out of 5.

    How big is a Bernese Mountain Dog compared with a 44-inch bed?

    The American Kennel Club breed profile gives adult males 25 to 27.5 inches at the shoulder and 80 to 115 pounds, and females 23 to 26 inches and 70 to 95 pounds. The breed standard also describes Berners as slightly longer in body than they are tall. Put simply, the body alone is longer than the dog is tall, and then you add the neck and head in front.

    That is why shoulder height and weight are poor guides for a bed. What matters is the length from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail. In a Wamiz owner forum thread on choosing bed size, the advice is to take that measurement and add 15 to 20 cm, about 6 to 8 inches, for the bed length, because two dogs of the same weight can be built very differently. The K9 of Mine dog bed size guide adds a useful detail for sprawlers: measure the legs too, and if you are between two sizes, choose the bigger one.

    How to measure a Berner who will not hold still

    • Wait until your dog is lying stretched out and relaxed, often right after a walk.
    • Lay a piece of string from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail, not the tail tip.
    • Measure the string with a tape. Accuracy within an inch or two is enough.
    • If your dog sleeps with legs straight out, also measure one front leg from elbow to toes.

    If your Berner is still growing, measure again every few weeks. The months covered in our guide to the Bernese teenage phase are a good time to re-measure, because a bed that fitted a few months earlier can suddenly look small.

    The three setups, one by one

    The selling point of the All-in-One is that the cushion lifts out of the bolster frame. That gives three ways to use the same bed.

    1. Whole bed: cushion in, low sides

    This is the default. The cushion sits inside the frame and the sides stay low, so a dog can lie across the bed and rest a head on the edge. For a Berner this is the setup that gives the most usable length, because a low edge does not push the dog's nose or paws back toward the middle. A dog who does not fit straight can often fit on the diagonal: corner to corner, a 44 x 34 inch rectangle is about 55.6 inches.

    2. Bolster bed: deeper sides for nesting

    The retail description calls this the burrowing setup. It suits the Berner who circles three times and presses a back into something solid. The trade-off is space: deeper sides take more of the 44 inches, so a long side sleeper will feel it here first.

    3. Cushion alone: the portable pad

    Lifted out, the cushion becomes a pad for the couch, a second room or a car. If you are working out whether a crate and a bed both fit in the back of your vehicle, our Bernese crate in an SUV fit checklist walks through the cargo measurements. Measure the cushion itself before you buy a crate around it, because its own size is not published separately.

    Washing a bed that lives under a shedding double coat

    Berners carry a thick double coat, and the bed collects it. The care label on the Scheels listing is more precise than the "machine wash and dry" headline: the filled pillow can go in the washer on a cold, gentle cycle, with non-chlorine bleach only, and should be tumble dried on low. The foam base should not be put into water.

    In practice that means the part your dog lies on can be washed, while the base is a wipe-and-vacuum job. Before the first wash, check that an XL cushion fits your washer drum with room to move; an overstuffed cushion washed in a drum that is too small comes out with dirty patches and a lot of hair still inside. A large-capacity machine at a laundromat is a fair option a few times a year.

    • Before washing: vacuum or use a rubber glove over the surface to lift loose undercoat. Hair left in the wash clogs filters and sticks to the fabric.
    • Drying: low heat, and reshape the cushion as the label says. Fully dry before it goes back in the frame.
    • Between washes: a washable throw on top catches most of the hair and is far easier to wash than the cushion.

    Less hair in the bed starts with brushing. The coat routine in our Bernese groomer instructions (weekly brushing, a full blow-out of loose undercoat, no shaving) also means less to vacuum off the bed.

    Pros and cons for Bernese owners

    What works

    • Three setups from one bed: low sides, deeper bolster, or the cushion alone.
    • The cushion washes in a home machine on a gentle cycle and can be tumble dried.
    • Low-sided setup lets a big dog use the diagonal and rest a head on the edge.
    • Soft microfiber that many dogs like for nesting.

    What to watch

    • 44 inches can be short for a big male who sleeps fully stretched out.
    • No inside dimensions or weight limit published in the listings we read.
    • The foam base must not be submerged, so it is not fully washable.
    • Microfiber holds shed undercoat; plan to vacuum often.

    Who should buy it, and who should skip it

    A good match if

    • Your Berner curls up or sleeps against something, and measures under about 36 inches nose to tail base.
    • You want one bed for the living room and a cushion for the car or a second room.
    • You are happy to wash the cushion and vacuum the base.

    Skip it if

    • Your dog sleeps flat on the side with legs out and the checker shows a negative margin.
    • You need a bed where every part goes in the washing machine.
    • Your teenage Berner is still destroying bedding. A cheaper pad makes more sense until that stage passes.

    A bed will not fix a dog who is tired of walks or refuses to move in the heat, but a comfortable place to rest after a walk helps. If that is your situation, our plan for a Berner who refuses to walk looks at the reasons first.

    Final verdict: is the Tall Tails All-in-One XL right for a Bernese Mountain Dog?

    3.6 / 5

    A versatile, washable-cushion bed that fits most curling and leaning Berners well and doubles as a travel pad. It is a gamble for the biggest males who sleep stretched out, because 44 inches leaves little room once the bolster is in place. Measure first and use the checker above.

    For a curler, it is an easy recommendation. For a long side sleeper, buy it only if the checker shows a few inches of margin or you can see the bed in a store.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does the Tall Tails All-in-One XL fit a Bernese Mountain Dog?

    It fits many Berners, especially dogs that curl up or lean against the sides. The XL is 44 x 34 inches on the outside. Measure your dog from nose to the base of the tail and add about 6 to 8 inches; if the result is over 44, a side-sleeping Berner will probably feel cramped.

    What are the Tall Tails All-in-One bed sizes?

    Three sizes: S/M, Large and XL. Retailers list the Large as 35 x 27 inches and the XL as 44 x 34 inches.

    Is the Tall Tails All-in-One bed machine washable?

    The filled cushion is: wash it cold on a gentle cycle, use only non-chlorine bleach and tumble dry low. The care label says the foam base should not be put in water, so spot clean and vacuum it.

    What are the three setups?

    The whole bed with the cushion in and low sides, a bolster bed with deeper sides for dogs that like to nest, and the cushion alone as a portable pad for the couch, another room or the car.

    How much does the Tall Tails All-in-One bed cost?

    The suggested retail price across the three sizes is $99.99 to $189.00. We saw the Large at $139.99 at Scheels, with the XL at the top of the range.

    Should I buy the Large for a Bernese puppy?

    Only if you are fine replacing it. At 35 x 27 inches, the Large suits a young puppy, but a Berner heading for adult size will outgrow it. Buying the XL once and adding a folded blanket for the puppy months is the cheaper route.

    Is it an orthopedic bed?

    Tall Tails does not market the All-in-One as orthopedic in the descriptions we read. It is described as an overstuffed bed with a foam base and a soft microfiber surface.

    Measure tonight, decide tomorrow

    The Tall Tails All-in-One Dog Bed is a thoughtful design, and for many Bernese Mountain Dogs the XL will be the spot they claim first. The only way to know is a piece of string and two minutes while your dog sleeps. If the numbers work, you get a bed, a nest and a travel cushion in one. If they do not, choose a longer flat bed and spend the difference on a good throw.
