When Do Bernese Mountain Dog Puppies Stop Biting? A Month-by-Month Timeline and What Actually Works

Home›Guides›Bernese puppy biting timeline

When do Bernese Mountain Dog puppies stop biting? General puppy guidance says most pups grow out of play biting at three to five months, yet Berner owners regularly report gentle mouthing well past their dog's first birthday. The difference matters, because the puppy who learns a soft mouth now becomes the 100-pound adult your neighbors, guests and their children are glad to meet.

Quick answer: Expect the sharpest, most frequent biting between about 8 weeks and 5 months, a clear drop for many Berners once the adult teeth are in at around 6 to 7 months, and soft, mouthy grabbing that can linger to 12 to 18 months in this breed. Biting fades fastest when you redirect to toys, prevent overtiredness, end play calmly after hard bites, and never punish. Stiff, frustrated biting during handling is a different problem and needs a professional.

    Why do Bernese puppies bite so much?

    Because mouthing is how puppies play and explore, and Berner puppies are large, busy and full of needle-sharp baby teeth. It is normal learning, not a sign of a bad dog.

    The UK charity Blue Cross explains that puppies investigate the world with their mouths and try to play with people the way they played with their littermates and mother. It adds that puppies from working breeds often seem to play bite more or harder, probably because they tend to be more energetic. That matches how many Berner owners describe their very physical, mouthy puppies.

    Overtired, not defiant

    In the most upvoted reply in a Bernese owner thread titled "Biting is breaking my spirit" (more than 1,700 upvotes and 124 comments), one owner compared the evening frenzy to a toddler throwing a tantrum because they are overtired before falling asleep. Blue Cross makes the same point: puppies need a lot of sleep, can get excitable or irritable when overtired, and hunger can also trigger mouthing. That is why the witching hour is often the worst hour.

    Why the teeth matter

    According to PetMD's puppy teething guide, puppies have a full set of 28 baby teeth by about 6 to 8 weeks, begin losing them at around 3½ to 5 months, and most have their full set of 42 adult teeth by about 6 months, with adult molars arriving at 5 to 7 months. PetMD also notes that between 3 and 7 months, puppies naturally explore with their mouths while teeth are changing. Owners often describe a noticeable improvement once that changeover is done.

    When do Bernese puppies stop biting? A month-by-month timeline

    Most Berners bite hardest and most often before 5 months, improve clearly around 6 to 7 months, and may stay softly mouthy until 12 to 18 months. Treat the dates as ranges, not deadlines.

    Why Berners often run late

    Blue Cross's three-to-five-month window applies to "the vast majority of puppies." Bernese owners describe something slower. In the r/bernesemountaindogs thread "end in sight?" (archived comments, JSON), the top reply says the behavior is very common for the breed and most grow out of it around a year and a half. Other owners in the same discussions report a big drop after 7 months, real improvement around 10 to 11 months, and one owner who has raised several Berners says theirs stopped anywhere from two to 10 months of age. The honest summary: hard biting usually ends far earlier than soft mouthing.

    What owners mean by "still mouthy"

    Almost every owner who reports mouthing past a year describes it as gentle: grabbing an arm "super gently," tugging sleeves, or lunging at a leash when overstimulated without trying to hurt. That is the payoff of good early work. A Berner who learned bite inhibition as a puppy still has a soft mouth when excited as a big adolescent, and that is the dog visitors praise as a gentle giant.

    What actually works to stop Bernese puppy biting?

    Redirect to toys, prevent the triggers, end play calmly after hard bites, and stay consistent across the household. Punishment makes biting worse, and some popular tips, like yelping, divide the experts.

    The ASPCA's mouthing guide sets the first goal as bite inhibition: teaching the puppy that human skin is sensitive, so any mouthing is gentle, before teaching that teeth do not belong on skin at all. It also warns that slapping or hitting a puppy for playful mouthing can make it bite harder and can even cause real aggression.

    Petting without being chewed

    The ASPCA suggests feeding small treats from one hand while you stroke with the other, so the puppy gets used to being touched without mouthing. For a coated breed that will spend a lifetime being brushed, that habit pays off later; our guide to briefing your Bernese groomer explains why calm handling matters so much at the salon.

    Feet, ankles and clothing

    When the puppy ambushes your feet, the ASPCA advice is to stop moving, offer a tug toy, and move again once the toy is in the mouth. Blue Cross adds a practical touch: loose clothing, fluffy slippers and belted dressing gowns that flap as you walk make you irresistible, so rethink your wardrobe for a few months.

    How do you keep children safe with a mouthy Berner puppy?

    Supervise every interaction and separate puppy and children whenever you cannot watch. Children should never be expected to manage play biting on their own.

    Blue Cross is direct about this: puppies have sharp teeth, children's reactions can encourage more mouthing, and stair gates or crates help create separate spaces. With a breed that can reach the size of an adult before it is mentally grown up, the habits you set now protect your family's reputation with every child who visits.

    House rules that work for families

    • Children sit on the floor or sofa to greet the puppy; no chasing games until mouthing is under control.
    • A toy goes into the puppy's mouth before hands go near it.
    • If teeth touch skin, the child stands still and an adult calmly takes over.
    • Rough wrestling with hands stays off the menu, as the ASPCA recommends noncontact games like fetch and tug instead.

    Is it play biting or something more serious?

    Play biting comes with a loose, wiggly body. Biting with a stiff body, especially when the puppy is held or handled, is a frustration "tantrum" that the ASPCA says a puppy will not simply grow out of.

    The ASPCA advises staying calm and unemotional during a tantrum, avoiding the yelp, and then arranging help from a qualified professional, such as a certified applied animal behaviorist, a veterinary behaviorist or a certified professional dog trainer. Owners on our checker who choose "stiff body or tantrum when handled" are sent straight to that advice, because it is the one pattern where waiting it out is the wrong plan.

    Biting on walks

    Some owners describe a puppy who walks nicely, then suddenly gets overstimulated and grabs the leash or their legs partway through. Shorter, calmer outings can help more than longer ones. Our look at real Bernese daily walking routines shows how other owners pace walks, and if your youngster later plants their feet instead, see what to do when a Bernese refuses to walk.

    Your two-week biting reset, step by step

    1. Days 1 to 2: track it. Use the bite log above and note when bites happen, as Blue Cross suggests, so you can predict the worst times.
    2. Days 1 to 14: stash toys. Keep a long tug toy or chew in every room and in your pocket.
    3. Days 1 to 14: schedule naps. Put the puppy down for quiet time before the hour your log shows is worst. A comfortable rest spot helps; our Tall Tails bed review covers sizing for a growing Berner.
    4. Days 3 to 14: teach the trade. Every time teeth reach for you, offer the toy and praise the switch.
    5. Days 3 to 14: end play calmly. After a hard bite, stop, step away for 30 to 60 seconds or use a gated break, then resume.
    6. Days 5 to 14: practice calm handling. Treat with one hand while touching ears, paws and coat with the other.
    7. Day 14: compare the log. A falling count means the plan works; a stiff-bodied, frustrated pattern means it is time for professional help.

    Common mistakes that make biting last longer

    • Playing with fingers. Blue Cross warns against encouraging play biting with fingers, however cute it looks at 9 weeks.
    • Jerking hands away. The ASPCA notes this encourages the puppy to lunge and grab; go limp instead.
    • Waving fingers or slapping the face to start play. Both teach the puppy that hands are toys.
    • Punishing. Hitting, scruff shaking or scolding can make biting harder and damage trust.
    • Skipping naps. An overtired Berner puppy is the bitiest Berner puppy.
    • Expecting a deadline. Giving up at 5 months because "it should have stopped" wastes the progress you made. In this breed, soft mouthing can outlast puppyhood, as our Bernese teenage phase guide explains.

    Bernese puppy biting FAQ

    At what age do Bernese puppies stop biting?

    Blue Cross says most puppies grow out of play biting at three to five months, but Bernese owners often report a clear drop around 6 to 7 months and gentle mouthing that can last to 12 to 18 months. Hard biting usually ends much earlier than soft mouthing.

    Is it normal for a Bernese puppy to bite this much?

    Yes. Mouthing is how puppies play and explore, and Blue Cross notes that puppies from working breeds often play bite more or harder. Large owner threads describe the same "land shark" phase in Berners.

    Does teething make Bernese puppies bite more?

    Teeth are changing at the same time. PetMD says puppies start losing baby teeth at about 3½ to 5 months and most have their adult teeth by about 6 months, and many owners notice improvement once the changeover is done.

    Should I yelp when my puppy bites?

    Experts disagree. The ASPCA suggests a high-pitched yelp and a limp hand for hard bites, no more than three times in 15 minutes, while Blue Cross advises against squealing because it can excite puppies. If yelping makes your puppy wilder, stop using it.

    What should I never do to stop puppy biting?

    Avoid hitting, scruff shaking, whacking the nose, putting fingers down the throat or scolding. The ASPCA warns that physical punishment can make puppies bite harder and can cause real aggression.

    When is puppy biting a problem that needs a professional?

    When the puppy bites with a stiff body or throws a tantrum while being held or handled. The ASPCA says this kind of frustration biting is not something a puppy simply grows out of.

    The soft mouth you build now lasts a lifetime

    When do Bernese Mountain Dog puppies stop biting? Hard nips usually fade by 6 to 7 months, and gentle mouthing can hang on until 12 to 18 months in this breed. Redirect, rest, calm breaks and patience are what get you there, and the reward is a big, gentle Berner that friends, neighbors and children are always happy to greet.
